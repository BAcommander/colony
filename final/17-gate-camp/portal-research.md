# Stargate reference notes for Gate Camp

Reviewed 2026-11-04. Gate Camp is a fixed wide shot of an already open gate seen from an expedition canopy. The screen version of SG‑1 is the main reference. The 1994 production notes help explain the liquid and reflection idea. Sources are used for design direction. No shot timings or colour values were derived from episode playback.

| Question | What the original crew or production material says | What to try in Gate Camp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How does the steady surface differ from opening? | SG‑1 VFX supervisor John Gajdecki describes shooting the expanding activation burst in a water tank. VFX producer James Tichenor explains how they filmed several angles and blended the practical element with the computer-generated puddle. The pilot's draft script treats the established surface like a pond when a character touches it. [Pilot crew oral history](https://www.thecompanion.app/stargate-sg-1-children-of-the-gods-oral-history/) · [Tichenor interview excerpt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pU06FiDUM4A) · [Pilot script](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/stargate-sg-1-101-children-of-the-gods-1997.pdf) | Start in the stable open state and keep the motion inside the ring. |
| What matters in a wide shot? | Cinematographer Jim Menard commissioned a loop for the practical projection first used for Thor's scene in SG‑1. Larger screens eventually made it practical for full gate shots. He reserved the more detailed effects for close views and crossing shots. [Menard's account](https://josephmallozzi.com/2008/08/13/august-13-2008-special-guest-blogger-stargate-director-of-photography-jim-menard-your-favorite-ronon-line-and-the-weird-food-purchase-of-the-day/) | Give the surface an easy-to-read, slow roll and keep the smaller texture underneath. |
| How does the gate feel connected to the set? | Gaffer Rick Dean explains the use of flickering blue light to suggest an active gate, even off camera. The *Full Circle* production feature shows director Martin Wood demonstrating a reflected-light rig aimed down onto the set floor. [Dean interview](https://www.rdanderson.com/stargate/features/articles/2002-09-rickdean.htm) · [Production feature transcript](https://www.stargate-sg1-solutions.com/wiki/Directors_Series:_6.22_%22Full_Circle%22) | Use the same moving pattern for the portal and a soft blue spill on nearby stone. |
| Where did the watery look come from? | 1994 digital effects artist Ed Kramer shared Frank Vitz's production sketches and notes. Vitz distinguished the sudden release of energy from the quieter, reflective surface once it settles. [Original notes and film still](https://www.thecompanion.app/stargate-rare-documents/) · [MGM film clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z1PS43VNDLw) | Retain the existing blue, deepen parts of the surface, and let pearl-blue highlights roll through it. |

Gate Camp already starts with the gate open. V2 treats the surface as a contained sheet of moving water throughout this audition. The application of soft highlights and landing spill is our interpretation of the production principles. The episode and film material serves as reference.
