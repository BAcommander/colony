# Weather and sound for Skies of Baal

Guy Haley's *The Devastation of Baal* distinguishes the planet from its moons. Chapter two describes airborne sand at the Arx Angelicum on Baal and a sudden warm wind coming out of the desert during an eclipse. Chapter one calls wind and dust storms the usual weather on Baal Secundus, and treats the still, toxic Red Mist there as unusual. The official Horus Heresy extract likewise describes the Baal system as devastated and Baal Secundus as desert. [The Devastation of Baal, Black Library](https://www.blacklibrary.com/authors/guy-haley/the-devastation-of-baal-ebook.html) · [Official Blood Angels background extract](https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/v5vlerjg/pages-from-the-black-books-the-blood-angels/)

The passages checked do not establish how often ordinary rain falls on Baal. The novel's lightning around the Arx during the siege is caused by weapons disturbing the atmosphere. For this quiet vigil, a dry terrace and windblown dust fit the setting. The far-off thunder is a creative choice for the clouds already visible in our scene.

| Priority | Layer | Direction for a source or mix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Desert wind below the terrace | Start by auditioning the library's [soft exterior wind](../../music/sfx-library/assets/wind-soft-exterior-01.mp3). Shape it as open air beyond the parapet, with a low steady floor and unequal gusts that rise and recede. Shelter the listening position without making it sound like an enclosed room. |
| 2 | Windblown mineral grit | Fine dry sand skimming over rough stone in a brief, soft pass. A few grains reach the wall as the gust peaks, then the texture clears. Avoid footsteps, snow crunch, piercing hiss and heavy debris. Audition it with wind before adding another layer. |
| 3 | Distant rolling thunder | A broad, diffuse roll beyond the mesas. Slow onset, slightly uneven body, long soft tail and little sharp treble. Try more than one take. Delay it a few seconds after a selected distant flash. Leave many flashes silent so the sound does not announce the one-minute visual loop. |
| 4 | Terrace lantern, optional | If the lamp is treated as a flame, try a tiny wick flutter under a gust. Keep it close and short. A continuous fireplace sound would imply a much larger source. |
| 5 | Far monastery bell, optional | One low, softened bell tail from deep in the complex, with long gaps. Treat it as an imagined detail for this terrace. Leave it out if it pulls attention from the music or reads like a timed cue. |

Build the first audition as music, exterior wind, grit and one distant thunder roll. Keep the layers separate. Follow the Snowline practice of checking the quiet passage and loudest gust at the same playback level, and keep grit tied to gusts while spacing thunder across multiple visual repeats. Let the soundscape breathe between events. [Snowline sound design notes](../../creative/music/sound-design/receiver-2026-11-02/REUSE.md)

The [V3 preview](../../music/beneath-a-crimson-sky-animation-v3/README.md) retains visual flashes at 0:08.4, 0:33.2 and 0:56.2 of each minute. Use these as possible cues, selecting different flashes across different repeats.

The [persistent storm audition](../../music/beneath-a-crimson-sky-persistent-storm/README.md) tests the stronger direction requested afterward: continuous wind, recurring grit, seven visual events and six thunder rolls in one effects-only minute. Keep the wind and grit separate from the thunder so we can maintain the storm floor and vary the rolls in the full mix.

The [synced audition](../../music/beneath-a-crimson-sky-synced-storm/README.md) builds two denser squalls into the larger strikes. It holds the dust and wind at crest through each nearby roll, then lets them recede. This timing is for the one-minute test; vary the recurrence of stronger squalls in a long assembly.
