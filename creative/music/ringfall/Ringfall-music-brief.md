## ElevenLabs prompt — closest evidence-based starting point

Instrumental deep drone ambient. A dark, bass-rich, immersive soundscape with a persistent low tonal foundation and extremely slow changes in texture. Deep rounded bass drones and soft sustained synthesizer layers, concentrated in the bass and low mids. Muted upper frequencies, smooth edges, no bright sparkling treble. A stable low E tonal center with open intervals and ambiguous harmony; avoid a recognizable chord progression. Soft overlapping tones slowly emerge and dissolve above the foundation. Broad diffuse stereo space, long dark reverb, gentle detuning and gradual filter movement. Restrained swells, consistent density, no dramatic climax. Free-time and beatless: no drums, percussion, rhythmic bassline or sequenced arpeggios. No vocals, choir, piano melody, bells or prominent lead. Maintain a continuous atmosphere throughout, with compatible opening and closing textures for a long crossfade. No final cadence or fade to silence.

## Generation and long-form assembly

1. Start with a 2–3 minute instrumental generation. Judge its tonal weight and rate of change before investing in a long arrangement.
2. If it is too melodic: “Reduce the foreground melody; sustained tones and slow timbral changes only.” If too bright: “Darker low-pass tone, softer upper frequencies, fewer high notes.” If too ominous: “Warm inhabited interior, gentle consonance, no threatening swells.”
3. Extend a successful palette into several related sections. Keep the tonal center and bass weight compatible. Check each section for unwanted percussion, vocal-like sounds, sudden events, and premature endings.
4. Assemble a longer master section, for example 10–20 minutes, before repeating it for a longer video. This is a suggested workflow, not the measured loop length of the reference.
5. Try 15–30 second overlaps as an editing starting point. Match tonal content and level before crossfading; choose the fade curve by listening because correlated drones can create bumps or cancellation. “Seamless loop” in a generation prompt is an intention, not a technical guarantee.
6. Keep room ambience separate if practical so its level can be adjusted without regenerating the music. Fade in once at the beginning of the finished video and fade out once at the end.
7. Check the final result on headphones, ordinary speakers, and in mono. Leave export headroom rather than reproducing the source's reconstructed overshoots.

ElevenLabs recommends specifying genre, mood, instruments, tempo behavior, and production characteristics, and explicitly requesting instrumental-only music. Its documentation also supports section-based development. Maximum-duration statements currently differ between official pages, so the 2–3 minute recommendation deliberately does not rely on a disputed maximum.
