## Complete generation prompt

Inside a geothermal heat-exchange hall supplying a small upland colony. This is practical thermal infrastructure: one insulated cylindrical buffer vessel on a short plinth, two main pipes with accessible shutoff valves, one pump skid and a clear dry maintenance aisle. An annular service gallery runs around the hall on visible columns, with one stair joining both levels. The building feels large enough for maintenance but not like an impossible cathedral of machinery.

The hall is partly cut into pale green-grey volcanic tuff with broad porous seams, while structural loads are carried by a concrete retaining ring and steel ribs. Overhead, a shallow circular daylight court is enclosed by a segmented glazed roof with mullions and drainage. Through it, short native stems support stiff overlapping russet-grey ribbons that twist once along their length, repeated as a single plant family across the upland. It is an alien low canopy, not a redwood forest opening through a roof. A service tunnel seen through an open interior doorway connects toward the colony's inhabited heat-distribution corridor, where two warm residential thresholds are visible beyond the utility zone.

Keep a narrow drained cooling trench at the hall perimeter; its water is separated from the dry vessel platform and protected electrical cabinets. No unexplained standing water across the whole floor. Signs of maintenance are limited to a closed toolbox and a folded pair of gloves beside a subdued gauge panel. No magic reactor core, glowing energy orb or enormous exposed turbine.

Camera from the upper gallery landing, natural 35 mm perspective, showing the vessel, a portion of the lower aisle and the daylight oculus in one understandable section. Soft overcast green-grey light from the roof contrasts with warm work fixtures below the gallery. Show quiet broad material surfaces and the human scale of handrails. The setting conveys the ordinary shared warmth that keeps a colony alive.

Render one original landscape 16:9 photographic location still for Ambient Colony, without lettering. This is an inhabited human colony on an unfamiliar world, maintained and working, with nobody visible. Use the described camera position and consistent human scale. Let supports, doorways, seals, maintenance access and service routes explain how the place works. Place only the few props specified; leave other surfaces quiet.

Keep painted metal, rubber, concrete, fabric and glass visually distinct, with modest wear at contact or weather-exposed areas. Preserve natural shadows, soft highlight roll-off and atmospheric loss of distant detail. The unusual environment should look observed through a real lens, with irregular natural structure rather than uniformly sharp procedural texture. No plastic gloss, exaggerated HDR, sharpening halos, duplicated props, warped furniture, decorative machinery or arbitrary extra cables. No people, spacecraft flybys, holograms, lettering, branding, fictional insignia, watermark, screenshot controls or borders. If reference images are supplied, use only their lighting, human scale and framing principles; do not copy their scene, Earth geography, titles or logos.

## Review before selection

Check stair continuity, bearing supports, pipe endpoints and dry electrical zones. Environment visible through the glazing must retain its native ribbon-growth identity.
