## Complete generation prompt

Look from a dry raised control gallery across a working tidal transfer lock on an ocean planet. The room has a curved pressure-rated end wall with several manageable reinforced window panels, not an unsupported single glass dome. A simple console with two dim displays sits at the lower right, with a chair and a neatly coiled maintenance lead. The chamber beneath the windows is a separate lower level behind watertight concrete walls and guardrails. No sea water reaches the operator floor.

Beyond the lock, a narrow slate-teal inlet passes between broad pale mineral shelves whose rounded undercut edges expose darker horizontal layers. Their upper surfaces carry low jointed growths made of dull lilac-grey blades that branch in consistent forked fans; keep them sparse and small. The landscape's repeated low overhangs and the misty green-grey sea distinguish it from a wooded Earth gorge. There are no giant crystals or glowing water. A simple outer flood gate is visible across the inlet, with a clear opening, hinged machinery housings and believable abutments.

On the far shore, a compact colony landing hall and two linked residential wings stand on competent rock above the high-water line. Their enclosed access bridge joins real doorways; insulated utility trunks run along one supported side. Mooring bollards and an empty recessed berth explain the lock's purpose without adding a boat. A wet lower service apron has drainage, while handrails and stairs lead continuously up to safe dry levels.

Use a 35 mm human-eye-height perspective from the gallery doorway. Diffuse teal-grey daylight enters from the inlet; warm-white interior work lights and amber exterior threshold lamps form small local pools. Leave a broad water region and quiet wall panels. The feeling is dependable shelter at the watery entrance to a small inhabited world.

Render one original landscape 16:9 photographic location still for Ambient Colony, without lettering. This is an inhabited human colony on an unfamiliar world, maintained and working, with nobody visible. Use the described camera position and consistent human scale. Let supports, doorways, seals, maintenance access and service routes explain how the place works. Place only the few props specified; leave other surfaces quiet.

Keep painted metal, rubber, concrete, fabric and glass visually distinct, with modest wear at contact or weather-exposed areas. Preserve natural shadows, soft highlight roll-off and atmospheric loss of distant detail. The unusual environment should look observed through a real lens, with irregular natural structure rather than uniformly sharp procedural texture. No plastic gloss, exaggerated HDR, sharpening halos, duplicated props, warped furniture, decorative machinery or arbitrary extra cables. No people, spacecraft flybys, holograms, lettering, branding, fictional insignia, watermark, screenshot controls or borders. If reference images are supplied, use only their lighting, human scale and framing principles; do not copy their scene, Earth geography, titles or logos.

## Review before selection

Trace the flood threshold, lock walls, gate hinge and safe access levels. The portal is a real structure, not a hole through which the sea floods the room.
