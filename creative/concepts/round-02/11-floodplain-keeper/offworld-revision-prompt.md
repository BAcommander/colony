## Exact proposed edit prompt

Use case: precise-object-edit / environmental revision.
Input image: concept-v3.png, the existing Floodplain Keeper control room. Preserve its composition, camera position, furniture, workstation, equipment, handleless ceramic beaker, restrained materials and soft photographic lighting. Primarily revise the view through all exterior glazing so the location unmistakably belongs to an off-world human colony.

This is the flood-management and water-reclamation station of a small human settlement on a distant, habitable planet with an atmosphere and liquid surface water. The room is ordinary, useful infrastructure built by colonists and maintained over many years. It remains occupied but empty of visible people.

Outside, preserve the functional sluice gate and flooded channels. Replace the Earth-like tree-lined horizon with low, dark, horizontally layered mineral escarpments softened by atmospheric haze. Replace the familiar reed beds with sparse, muted blue-grey marsh growth of consistent botanical form: low jointed stalks, clustered narrow leaves and patches of pale mineral sediment. Keep its scale and density believable; no glowing plants or giant vegetation.

On the far bank, add a small connected cluster of three or four practical prefabricated colony modules, linked by enclosed service passages and a raised access causeway. Show modest habitation windows and one simple communications mast. Keep the settlement clearly readable but secondary to the control room and sluice. Perspective, elevation and access must agree with the flood level.

In a thin break in the grey cloud cover, show one pale cratered moon, roughly one tenth of the main window's width. It is low-contrast, partly veiled by atmosphere and clearly separate from the terrain. No other celestial objects. Its visibility must agree with the clouds and daylight exposure.

Retain grey overcast daylight and one modest warm desk lamp. The colony modules use restrained practical lights. The room and landscape share the same light direction, atmospheric conditions and exposure. Maintain natural depth, matte materials, quiet surfaces and physically plausible construction. Preserve window frames and foreground geometry; include the revised exterior consistently through the door glazing and in any relevant reflections.

Mood: a quiet shift keeping a small colony supplied with clean water, far from Earth. The place feels inhabited, dependable and remote. Science fiction comes from the setting and the purpose of the infrastructure.

Avoid Earth-like deciduous trees, recognisable terrestrial landmarks, giant planets filling the window, multiple moons, spacecraft, neon, holograms, bioluminescence, decorative machinery, excessive grime, plastic gloss, exaggerated HDR or sharpened textures. No people, titles, branding, watermarks or interface graphics. Preserve landscape framing and output one complete photograph.

## Reusable addition for future location prompts

This is human-built infrastructure serving a small colony on a distant planet, not an Earth location. Use familiar, practical construction and equipment, but establish the off-world setting through a coherent planetary environment and visible evidence of a wider settlement. Choose two or three compatible cues: distinctive geology or vegetation, a restrained celestial feature, and connected colony infrastructure. Do not pile every possible science-fiction motif into the frame. State whether the environment has an atmosphere, liquid water and vegetation so the imagery stays internally consistent.

Review at normal composition size: does it read as a colony location without relying on a title? Does the settlement visibly connect to the facility's function? Do light, scale, water level, atmosphere and the celestial feature agree? Preserve the current realistic room rather than replacing it with generic spaceship controls.
