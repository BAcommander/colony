2026-10-27. Brainstorm requested after positive Brineward v1 feedback.
Proposals only: no images generated, no catalog numbers, no changes to the earlier round-03 backlog. Working names can change.

SHARED APPROACH
Standing/seated camera with a place to stand; ordinary recognizable human materials; clear access and weather/pressure boundaries; large quiet areas; restrained light; one dominant unfamiliar environmental form, a consistent supporting cue and a practical construction response. Realism means coherent relationships, not extra texture.

REFERENCE COMPARISON
The supplied snowy station screenshot has a narrower hue range than Brineward: a strong blue/cyan cast covers most of the image, interrupted by a small bright warm opening. This is a visual observation, not measured colour analysis. Brineward is already subdued in saturation, but its grey/lilac basin, near-neutral station and cream light give more material colour variety. More blue does not automatically mean more realistic.
The reference's stair-to-dome route gives a single clear visual journey. Fog suppresses competing distant detail. Brineward has stronger explicit alien geology, but repeated sharp mineral blades and heavily detailed exposed surfaces spread attention. Neither image is objectively more interesting: the reference prioritizes anticipation and refuge, ours prioritizes unfamiliar terrain.
Next useful experiment: maintain the alien silhouette while reducing fine surface contrast and letting one route or refuge dominate. Do not blanket-desaturate every candidate, copy the reference's dome/stair layout, or treat our interpretation as proof of source provenance.

1. THE LEESIDE TERMINAL
Purpose: a small passenger transfer shelter for a colony tram.
View: eye level from beneath a plain concrete canopy; one bench, closed glazed shelter and a readable platform edge. One track enters a low tunnel.
Alien signature: huge wind-cut arching sediment ribs form a sheltered valley, with matching fractured rib material near the platform.
Adaptation/life: track and service line follow the protected lee; enclosed waiting room has one warm lamp and a folded maintenance coat.
Palette/weather: pale overcast, dusty mauve-grey terrain and quiet off-white construction; no wet pavement.
Learning test: can a dry daytime scene feel as convincing as a wet blue-hour scene? Keep rail/tunnel geometry simple.

2. UNDER THE GLASS SHELF
Purpose: heat-exchanger inspection gallery beneath a natural ice overhang.
View: from an enclosed side gallery, looking obliquely through sealed glazing past a small inspection station.
Alien signature: thick translucent sea-green ice shelves contain deep irregular opaque layers, hanging above a dark tidal fissure.
Adaptation/life: buildings anchored in rock independently of the ice; protected flexible service joints and a clearly dry occupied floor.
Palette/weather: diffuse green-white daylight, charcoal machinery, one ordinary cream task lamp.
Learning test: convincing transmitted light and thermal boundaries without glowing fantasy crystals or decorative frost indoors.

3. THE LOW-TIDE GAUGE HOUSE
Purpose: estuary flood-gate control and tide monitoring.
View: standing on an elevated maintenance deck beside a sealed gauge room.
Alien signature: a broad drained basin of cellular mineral terraces with dark brine retained inside irregular shallow cells.
Adaptation/life: anchored adjustable gate machinery, elevated access and a tide-stained exterior datum; a thermos behind glass.
Palette/weather: flat silver daylight, desaturated olive salts and grey concrete; distant squall obscures the basin edge.
Learning test: one low-relief alien texture visible at composition scale, with readable wet/dry boundaries and grounded mechanics.

4. THE FOLD BASIN DEPOT
Purpose: municipal stores and freight loading bay.
View: from a dry recessed loading shelter toward a short service road and closed depot doors.
Alien signature: enormous folded pale rock surfaces form long troughs with broad rounded crests, no pointed mountain skyline.
Adaptation/life: road and stores occupy stable trough margins; low grit barriers, two useful cargo pallets and a parked hand trolley.
Palette/weather: muted chalk and rust-grey dust under thick high cloud; no sunset.
Learning test: human use through layout and a few objects rather than banks of screens; avoid turning folds into fabric.

5. THE UMBER GROVE CLINIC
Purpose: a small settlement medical annex.
View: sheltered entrance approach with a level ramp and one lit reception window.
Alien signature: sparse native organisms have low, broad layered shell-like crowns on stout branching supports; visible examples vary in age and damage.
Adaptation/life: raised foundations clear the seasonal spore mat, filtered air intakes and washable entry surfaces.
Palette/weather: matte umber biology, pale grey buildings, soft neutral morning light; no luminous foliage.
Learning test: unfamiliar ecology that stays materially credible and quiet, without a forest of repeated identical assets.

6. THE SHADOWLINE RESERVOIR
Purpose: water storage and pumping for a settlement on a tidally locked world.
View: from a warm sealed pump-control bay across one restrained exterior window; sparse mechanical controls.
Alien signature: low grazing light follows a continuous ice front across a dark plain beneath an elongated stationary cloud bank.
Adaptation/life: buried insulated pipes, a protected route to the next occupied building and thermal shielding on exposed joints.
Palette/weather: near-neutral stone, muted copper grazing light and blue-grey shadow; no huge planet.
Learning test: legible alien climate through consistent illumination and construction rather than spectacle. Do not claim real orbital science from one image.

7. THE HOLLOW ESCARPMENT POST
Purpose: slope stability monitoring and road maintenance.
View: eye level from a simple roadside lay-by, with one low survey hut and a guarded instrument pad.
Alien signature: vast weathered rock buttresses contain irregular tall natural cavities; smaller fractured cavities expose the same material nearby.
Adaptation/life: station sits outside the fall zone, sheltered cabling reaches anchored instruments, a patched work jacket hangs inside.
Palette/weather: milk-grey haze, subdued violet-brown stone, one modest warm window.
Learning test: massive unfamiliar geometry with believable weight and collapse history; avoid a skyline of identical arches.

8. THE STILL AIR BALCONY
Purpose: atmospheric sampling station in an inhabited high-altitude settlement.
View: sheltered service balcony beside a sealed laboratory, looking across open air to two tethered utility structures.
Alien signature: a dense low cloud ocean carries broad dark atmospheric bands; distant settlement infrastructure establishes height.
Adaptation/life: balcony belongs to a visibly substantial supported structure; wind screens, secure equipment and enclosed access.
Palette/weather: subdued pearl daylight, slate bands, matte pale panels; no sunset or neon.
Learning test: unfamiliar atmosphere with enough context to read as inhabited engineering; do not hide impossible supports in fog.

9. THE WINTER SEED EXCHANGE
Purpose: food-growing district seed store and propagation service room.
View: seated human-height perspective from a plain potting bench through sealed glazing toward protected growing enclosures.
Alien signature: low flattened native cushion colonies make irregular raised islands across a frost plain, with coherent radial growth scars.
Adaptation/life: crops remain recognizably human within separate enclosed beds; clean storage, a few labelled-looking but unreadable trays and practical drainage.
Palette/weather: muted blue-green native ground, neutral daylight and a single warm side fixture.
Learning test: everyday habitation and quiet functional interiors without desk clutter or generic Earth plants relabelled alien.

10. THE LONG DUSK REPAIR SHED
Purpose: repair stop on a colony's service-vehicle route.
View: from beneath a sheltered work canopy, one open exterior service bay in front and a closed occupied cabin to one side.
Alien signature: a broad dark plain is divided by sparse tall tabular rock ridges, eroded in one consistent prevailing-wind direction.
Adaptation/life: vehicle-width route follows gaps, replaceable grit screens protect the bay; a single wheel assembly and work light suggest an unfinished shift.
Palette/weather: low dull peach-grey sky, almost neutral ground, restrained tungsten light; no amber cinematic sunset.
Learning test: the emotional promise of a stopping place and unfinished work without turning it into an equipment showcase.
