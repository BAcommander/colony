CHRISTMAS SOMEWHERE YOU WISH YOU COULD STAY
2026-10-27. Ten written scene proposals requested by user. No images generated; no release numbers, music or animation authorized by this brainstorming request.

CORE PROMISE
Warmth, shelter, a place to sit and a little Christmas away from home. The sci-fi identity makes familiar comfort distinctive. Each image needs a readable refuge and one seasonal anchor at thumbnail scale. A garland that disappears at list size cannot carry the whole Christmas idea.
Use roughly two or three purposeful festive details, no decorations on every surface. Maintain source-specific construction, hardware era and palette. Give cold/exposed exterior and warm/dry interior a clear boundary. Snow falls on planetary surfaces, not outside orbital windows. A conventional fireplace needs a plausible building/chimney; shipboard alternatives can be an enclosed electric fire or small radiant heater. These are scene choices, not new blanket restrictions.
Keep a visibly comfortable chair/table/bench in most compositions; do not turn every concept into a desk with a tiny tree. Original viewpoints and compositions; named influences are design context, no official affiliation. Emotional headline ideas below are untested thumbnail candidates, not actual publication titles or CTR evidence.

01. CHRISTMAS IN THE TIME MACHINE WORKSHOP
Influence: Back to the Future, with the DeLorean prominent as requested in the recent set.
View from an old comfortable armchair beside a small vented cast-iron workshop stove. The stainless time machine rests inside a garage, one gullwing door raised, cabin time circuits softly lit. Through the closed broad garage glazing, snow settles on a quiet 1980s suburban drive.
Christmas anchors: a short warm bulb string around the garage frame; one modest tree on the workbench end; a wrapped parcel on the passenger seat.
Hierarchy: inviting firelit seat -> unmistakable car -> snowy blue night. Stove/chimney stays separate from car and fuel/work materials; car wheels and door geometry get explicit review.
Thumbnail thought: HOME FOR CHRISTMAS.
Why this earns a place: a recognisable extraordinary object in an ordinary place you could actually spend an evening.

02. THREE MILLION CHRISTMASES FROM HOME
Influence: Red Dwarf, off-white angular bunkroom with red pipes and lived-in working-class possessions.
Warm room with two bunks, one plain meal table and a view through the octagonal doorway into the empty ship. A simple curry supper, tea and one occupied-looking but empty chair make the room the destination, not just a display of props.
Christmas anchors: a small slightly leaning artificial tree; two mismatched stockings on the wall beside a small enclosed electric heater; one handmade paper chain.
Hierarchy: humble meal and warm light -> tree -> huge loneliness beyond the porthole. No character face/Holly likeness or comic disaster needed.
Thumbnail thought: CHRISTMAS, A LONG WAY FROM HOME.
Watch for: avoid generic luxury spaceship cosy room; keep the affectionate scruffiness without universal dirt.

03. CHRISTMAS ON THE NIGHT WATCH
Influence: Star Trek TOS recreation lounge, not a decorated command bridge.
One curved midcentury sofa and a small table in a clean grey room with red door accents and coloured physical console buttons. A forward viewscreen shows a slow-looking snowy alien world as a still composition; a smaller actual observation port can show distant stars, clearly distinct from screen.
Christmas anchors: one modest tree with warm white and primary-colour bulbs; a red-and-gold folded throw; a little plate of biscuits.
Hierarchy: optimistic comfortable lounge -> festive tree -> strange world on screen. Broad colour blocks and smooth maintained materials, no modern blue hologram styling.
Thumbnail thought: CHRISTMAS ON THE FINAL FRONTIER.
Watch for: generous but believable room scale and intact sofa/door/control geometry.

04. THE SNOWBOUND GATE LODGE
Influence: Stargate SG-1 expedition kit beside ancient technology.
A small timber-and-metal expedition lodge on a winter planet. A vented wood stove, plain sofa and warm table lamp near a sealed panoramic window; outside a recognisable ancient ring stands on a snow-dusted plinth, with a restrained blue transit surface contrasting the window warmth.
Christmas anchors: a small real tree grown/supplied for the lodge; one garland around the interior beam; two coats and a modest parcel at the door.
Hierarchy: stove-lit refuge -> glowing ring -> broad snow and unfamiliar rock silhouettes. No enormous Earth forest or moons as the only alien cue.
Thumbnail thought: CHRISTMAS BEYOND THE GATE.
Watch for: real route from lodge to gate, continuous steps, sealed window, plausible chimney termination.

05. THE SALVAGE QUARTER CHRISTMAS WINDOW
Influence: Andor working-town masonry and community, building on the stronger salvage-district direction.
Inside a small upstairs flat over a closed repair shop, a worn armchair and ordinary stove face a wide window. Beyond: snow in a layered lane, warm upper-floor homes, and the giant partly stripped hull of a ship rising beyond the roofs.
Christmas anchors: one compact tree beside the chair; modest window lights repeated in only a few nearby homes; a warm meal on a side table.
Hierarchy: a room to come home to -> recognisable busy-lived-in district -> immense industry at rest.
Thumbnail thought: THE COLONY IS HOME TONIGHT.
Watch for: avoid adding so many golden windows that the street becomes a fantasy Christmas market.

06. THE FREIGHTER'S LITTLE CHRISTMAS
Influence: Alien 1979 commercial-space mess, cream padded seats, heavy hatch and green CRT bridge beyond.
An empty bolted dining nook with one warm functional light, modest supper and a padded bench. An open interior hatch leads toward a softly lit working corridor; a narrow sealed port shows a cold planet limb.
Christmas anchors: a tiny tree strapped securely to a cabinet shelf; two paper stars; one parcel on the table.
Hierarchy: modest recognisable crew meal -> small tree -> vast distance suggested by the port.
Thumbnail thought: A LITTLE CHRISTMAS IN DEEP SPACE.
Watch for: no creature/horror insinuation, decorative spacecraft snow, mansion fireplace or overload of glowing screens. Understatement is this candidate's point.

07. CHRISTMAS WITH THE FLEET
Influence: Battlestar Galactica civilian-fleet endurance, explicitly on a transport rather than a windowed CIC.
A small civilian mess-room corner, worn burgundy seats, practical metal table and an enclosed radiant heater beside the wall. Across a reinforced observation window a battered carrier and a few civilian ships hold formation.
Christmas anchors: a small handmade tree of spare wire and a few warm bulbs; cloth stockings with no names; a shared-looking supper laid out without people.
Hierarchy: intimate human table -> fragile-looking homemade celebration -> fleet still together outside.
Thumbnail thought: STILL TOGETHER THIS CHRISTMAS.
Watch for: keep decoration restrained enough to belong to resource-limited people; visual gravity/orientation coherent.

08. THE BELT'S CHRISTMAS CANTEEN
Influence: The Expanse's working asteroid settlements, exposed rock, retrofits and practical lighting.
A small canteen built against dark asteroid rock. One warm dining booth, an ordinary food counter and a short glazed route to homes. A dock window frames a stationary freighter with sparse cold work lamps.
Christmas anchors: a dwarf citrus tree in a reused planter decorated with a few paper ornaments; a short bulb string; warm food on one table.
Hierarchy: welcoming community booth -> improbable living tree -> huge docked ship.
Thumbnail thought: CHRISTMAS IN THE BELT.
Watch for: keep the living tree distinct from a shipboard jungle, pressure boundary clear, no snow outside in vacuum.

09. WINTER AT THE RAINWARD KEEP
Influence: Dune's cold coastal architectural strand, rather than importing Christmas into another generic desert hut.
A comfortable chair, simple woven blanket and contained fire in a massive dark stone hearth. A deeply recessed sealed window looks onto a storm coast, snow on high mineral cliffs and cold rain/sleet lower down. The building's mass makes the refuge feel substantial.
Christmas anchors: a small tree with a few warm lights; one plain evergreen arrangement on the hearth side shelf; two parcels.
Hierarchy: firelit chair -> deep window reveal -> violent vast ocean. Original keep and hearth design, not a copied film set.
Thumbnail thought: CHRISTMAS ABOVE THE WINTER SEA.
Watch for: do not flood all the stone with amber or leave the entire foreground black; hearth/flue, glazing and terrace supports must be coherent.

10. CHRISTMAS BENEATH THE RINGS
Influence: Ambient Colony's own Ringfall identity, as a new seasonal composition rather than an unapproved overwrite of the accepted artwork.
A small observatory living room with a broad sealed window, comfortable chair and enclosed electric fireplace set into a plain wall. Outside, immense planetary rings cross a cold alien horizon above an inhabited outpost.
Christmas anchors: a simple tree beside the window; a knitted blanket; warm window lights echoed by one nearby habitation.
Hierarchy: unmistakable rings -> warm room you can inhabit -> festive tree. The scene must work without giant text; keep the ring field visible.
Thumbnail thought: CHRISTMAS AT THE EDGE OF EVERYTHING.
Watch for: don't reuse approved motion masks or imply a new Ringfall edition is approved; keep all original masters unchanged.

SUGGESTED FIRST THREE
1. Time Machine Workshop: fastest recognizable nostalgia and a clear hero object.
2. Snowbound Gate Lodge: combines classic winter shelter with an unmistakable sci-fi threshold.
3. Christmas Beneath the Rings: distinctive channel identity with a strong festive refuge.
Alternative if the user wants humour and affectionate familiarity: Red Dwarf bunkroom.
These are creative priorities, not audience-performance predictions.
